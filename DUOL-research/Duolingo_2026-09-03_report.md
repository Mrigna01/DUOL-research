# Duolingo:

## Investment view

**Company:** Duolingo, Inc. (NASDAQ: DUOL). *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, cover page.)*

**Valuation date:** September 1, 2026. *(Source: author-supplied project date.)*

**Decision:** **Watch-defer.** I like Duolingo's business, but I am not convinced that the current price gives me enough protection against uncertainty in its normalized earnings. *(Source: author judgment.)*

The tension is straightforward. Duolingo is attracting more users, getting them to return more often, and converting more of them into paying subscribers. Revenue is growing faster than operating expenses, and the balance sheet is strong. At the same time, reported net income makes profitability look better than the underlying business alone produced, while the stock still appears to carry a demanding growth valuation. *(Sources: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 60-70; valuation characterization: author judgment; current valuation level: UNSOURCED.)*

## The business

Duolingo uses a freemium model. Most learners can use the product without paying, while the company earns money from paid subscriptions, advertising, in-app purchases, and the Duolingo English Test. Its subscription products are Super Duolingo and Duolingo Max. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 58-59.)*

The model works best when the free product creates a habit. A large, engaged audience gives Duolingo several ways to make money: some users subscribe, while free users can generate advertising and in-app-purchase revenue. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 58-61.)*

That is why I care about engagement as much as the total user count. A user who opens the app regularly is more valuable than someone who downloads it once and disappears. *(Source: author judgment.)*

## Why I like the business

### Engagement is improving, not merely expanding

During the three months ended December 31, 2025, Duolingo averaged 133.1 million monthly active users and 52.7 million daily active users. MAUs grew 14% from the comparable 2024 period, while DAUs grew 30%. The DAU-to-MAU ratio increased from 34.7% to 39.6%. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 60-61.)*

To me, DAUs growing faster than MAUs is evidence that the user base is becoming higher quality. People are not simply signing up; a larger share of monthly users is making Duolingo part of a daily routine. *(Source: author judgment based on Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 60-61.)*

Paid subscribers reached 12.2 million at December 31, 2025, up 28% from 9.5 million one year earlier. Paid subscribers represented 9.2% of average MAUs for 2025, compared with 8.8% for 2024. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 59-61.)*

That combination—more users, stronger daily engagement, and a higher paid-conversion rate—is the clearest part of the investment case for me. *(Source: author judgment.)*

### Revenue growth is producing operating leverage

Revenue increased 39%, from $748.0 million in 2024 to $1.038 billion in 2025. Subscription revenue rose 44% to $873.4 million, primarily because the average number of paid subscribers increased. Other revenue rose 17% to $164.1 million, mainly because higher DAUs led to more advertisements being served. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 68.)*

Total operating expenses grew 27%, slower than revenue. As a percentage of revenue, operating expenses declined from 64% to 59%, and operating margin increased from 8% to 13%. Operating income rose from $62.6 million to $135.6 million. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 67-69.)*

This is real operating leverage: the business added revenue faster than it added operating costs, so profitability scaled faster than the expense base. That is exactly what I want to see in a software-driven subscription company. *(Source: author judgment based on Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 67-69.)*

### Management has built credibility against its own guidance

Duolingo has exceeded the high end of its initial annual guidance for bookings, revenue, and adjusted EBITDA margin in both FY2024 and FY2025. The pattern appears meaningful rather than the result of narrowly clearing expectations. *(Source: UNSOURCED.)*

That two-year record gives me some confidence in management's ability to forecast and execute. It does not eliminate valuation risk, but it makes the operating performance harder to dismiss as a single unusually strong year. *(Source: author judgment; underlying guidance record: UNSOURCED.)*

## What the 2026 outlook adds

Management established a longer-term adjusted EBITDA margin target of 30%-35% in early 2024. FY2025 adjusted EBITDA margin reached 29.5%, leaving the company just below that range. *(Source for FY2025 margin: author calculation using adjusted EBITDA and revenue from Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 60 and 67; source for the longer-term target: UNSOURCED.)*

On the Q4 2025 earnings call, management guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA margin to approximately 25%. Leadership described the reduction as a deliberate trade-off: accepting lower near-term profitability to invest in faster user growth. Management also set a goal of reaching 100 million daily active users by 2028, roughly twice the user base at the time of the call. *(Source: Duolingo Q4 2025 earnings call, February 2026; exact call date and transcript link not supplied.)*

This decision cuts both ways for me. Reinvestment could widen Duolingo's lead and create more future subscribers, but it postpones the evidence of durable normalized earnings that I want before paying a premium multiple. A lower 2026 margin therefore strengthens my watch-defer position even if the spending is strategically sensible. *(Source: author judgment based on Duolingo Q4 2025 earnings call, February 2026.)*

Management also argued on that call that general-purpose AI tools such as ChatGPT were not taking meaningful share from Duolingo. Leadership cited an estimated 85% share of daily active users among language-learning apps and said users generally leave because they become busy, not because they switch to competing products. Analysts challenged that confidence by asking about softer viral and organic growth, Duolingo's acknowledged history of adding too much monetization friction, and whether moving Video Call into lower-priced subscription tiers could reduce demand for the higher-margin Max plan. *(Source: Duolingo Q4 2025 earnings call, February 2026; exact call date and transcript link not supplied.)*

I treat the 85% figure as management's estimate, not independent proof of competitive dominance. The analyst questions identify the more useful tests: whether growth remains organic, whether monetization damages the user experience, and whether broader access to premium features expands conversion or simply shifts subscribers into cheaper plans. *(Source: author judgment based on Duolingo Q4 2025 earnings call, February 2026.)*

Q2 2026 results showed DAU growth reaccelerating to 23% year over year, an improvement from Q1 2026. CEO Luis von Ahn also reiterated the company's long-term ambition to teach one billion people. *(Source: UNSOURCED.)*

The DAU improvement is encouraging because it suggests the growth investment may be supporting engagement. One quarter, however, is not enough for me to conclude that the return on that spending is durable or that it will translate into normalized earnings. *(Source: author judgment; underlying Q2 2026 result: UNSOURCED.)*

### The financial position provides breathing room

Duolingo ended 2025 with $1.036 billion of cash and cash equivalents, $104.1 million of short-term investments, and $135.1 million of long-term investments. The balance sheet reported no borrowing debt, although it did include operating-lease liabilities. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 70, and Item 8, p. 83.)*

Operating cash flow increased 36%, from $285.5 million to $387.8 million, while the company's non-GAAP free cash flow increased from $264.4 million to $360.4 million. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 60 and 71.)*

I view that liquidity as a meaningful safety margin. If growth slows, Duolingo has time and resources to adjust rather than facing an immediate financing problem. The statement that it has “years of runway” is my interpretation, not a forecast supplied by management. *(Source: author judgment based on Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 70-71, and Item 8, p. 83.)*

## Why I am not ready to buy

### Reported net income overstates recurring earnings

Duolingo reported $414.1 million of net income for 2025. That result included a one-time $256.7 million benefit caused by the release of a valuation allowance against deferred-tax assets. Management states that the resulting effective tax rate is not necessarily indicative of future periods. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 60, 62, and 70.)*

Subtracting that one-time benefit from reported net income produces approximately $157.4 million. I use that simple figure as an initial normalized-profit anchor because it better reflects what remains after removing the unusually large tax benefit. It is not a complete normalized earnings model and does not adjust every nonrecurring or noncash item. *(Source: author calculation using Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 60 and 70; interpretation: author judgment.)*

This distinction changes how I see the stock. The $414.1 million headline number makes earnings power look stronger than the recurring business currently demonstrates. I would rather value Duolingo against normalized profitability than give full credit to an accounting benefit that will not repeat. *(Source: author judgment based on Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 60 and 70.)*

### The apparent valuation is less attractive after normalization

The stock's trailing P/E appears to be approximately 19 times when calculated using reported GAAP earnings. *(Source: UNSOURCED.)*

On a forward-earnings basis, the stock appears to trade closer to 60 times earnings. *(Source: UNSOURCED.)*

Those figures may not be directly comparable because the underlying price, share count, earnings period, and analyst-estimate source were not supplied. They must be verified before being presented as investment-committee evidence. *(Source: author assessment; underlying valuation inputs: UNSOURCED.)*

Even provisionally, the valuation framing matters to me. A multiple around 60 times earnings is a growth-stock price, while my approach emphasizes value and downside protection. I want evidence of durable, normalized earnings power before paying a premium multiple; I do not want to pay today for growth that still has to be proven. *(Source: author judgment; referenced multiple: UNSOURCED.)*

### Gross margin is moving in the wrong direction

Gross margin declined from 72.8% in 2024 to 72.2% in 2025. Management attributed the decline to higher AI costs in subscription features such as Video Call and to a shift toward advertising revenue, which carries a lower margin than subscription revenue. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 68-69.)*

I do not dismiss this as noise. AI may improve the product and support a higher-priced subscription, but its cost could also become a continuing burden on the model. Until the margin stabilizes, I am not willing to assume that these costs are merely temporary. *(Source: author judgment.)*

There is also a brand-execution risk around AI. In 2025, the CEO's announcement of an “AI-first” strategy and internal AI-adoption expectations triggered public criticism and reports of subscription cancellations. By early 2026, he had softened parts of that message, clarifying that Duolingo was still hiring people and would not require AI usage in employee performance evaluations. *(Source: UNSOURCED.)*

This matters because Duolingo's brand and customer loyalty are central to its low-cost growth model. I do not view the controversy alone as a reason to reject the company, but repeated messaging mistakes or measurable cancellations would weaken the argument that its brand provides durable protection. *(Source: author judgment; underlying controversy and cancellation reports: UNSOURCED.)*

### Cash flow is strong, but dilution still matters

Duolingo recorded $137.4 million of stock-based compensation expense in 2025. Its adjusted EBITDA reconciliation excluded $148.6 million of stock-based compensation and related taxes on equity awards. The diluted weighted-average share count was 48.308 million, compared with 45.773 million basic weighted-average shares. *(Source: Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 62; Item 8, pp. 84 and 110.)*

Because stock-based compensation transfers part of the company's value to employees, I do not treat all reported free cash flow as though it belongs to existing shareholders without considering dilution. *(Source: author judgment.)*

- Whether several additional quarters show that revenue growth is becoming sustained normalized profit rather than relying on unusual tax effects, especially while management targets a lower FY2026 adjusted EBITDA margin. *(Source: author research requirement; approximately 25% FY2026 guidance from Duolingo Q4 2025 earnings call, February 2026.)*
- Whether gross margin stabilizes as Duolingo scales AI-enabled products, or whether AI remains a permanent drag on unit economics. *(Source: author research requirement.)*
- Whether sales and marketing can grow more slowly than revenue while user engagement and paid subscriptions continue expanding. Sales and marketing expense grew 39% in 2025, the same rate as revenue. *(Source: author research requirement; historical comparison from Duolingo 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 67-69.)*

## What would change my mind

I would become more constructive if Duolingo produced several more quarters of normalized earnings growth, stabilized gross margin or demonstrated that AI costs are a temporary investment phase, and either grew into the current valuation or traded at a multiple more consistent with my value-oriented requirement for downside protection. *(Source: author judgment.)*

## Final call: watch-defer

**My call is watch-defer: Duolingo is a strong company, but I am not ready to initiate a position at a growth valuation until the earnings quality and margin durability are clearer.** 

**What would change the call:** verified valuation support combined with several additional quarters of sustained normalized earnings growth and stable gross margin would move me toward initiation.
