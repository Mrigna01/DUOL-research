# Project 1 - Edition A: Duolingo, Inc. (NASDAQ: DUOL)

## Decision and user

This memo supports an investment committee deciding whether the fund should initiate a position in Duolingo. The decision is whether the available evidence supports committing capital now, continuing research without committing capital, or rejecting the opportunity.

## Company and valuation date

- Company: Duolingo, Inc. (NASDAQ: DUOL; CIK 0001562088)
- Valuation date: September 1, 2026
- Primary source reviewed: Duolingo's Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, accession 0001628280-26-012494, filed February 27, 2026 ([local filing](./DUOL-10-K.pdf); [SEC filing detail](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/0001628280-26-012494-index.htm))

## Initial thesis: watch / defer

Do not initiate yet. Duolingo shows attractive product-led growth, strong engagement, rising paid conversion, positive operating cash flow, and a debt-free balance sheet. Those facts justify continued research. They do not yet justify buying the stock because this Edition A has not established the market price, enterprise value, expectations embedded in that price, performance after December 2025, or whether growth can remain efficient as the business scales.

The investable thesis to test is that Duolingo's large and increasingly engaged free-user base creates a durable, low-acquisition-cost funnel into paid subscriptions. If paid conversion and retention compound without sales and marketing growing faster than revenue, revenue and cash flow may scale faster than the cost base. The counter-thesis is that engagement or subscriber growth normalizes while valuation continues to require exceptional growth, producing poor returns even if the company remains a good business.

## Known evidence

Unless otherwise noted, the evidence below comes from the 2025 Form 10-K cited above.

- Scale and engagement: for the three months ended December 31, 2025, average monthly active users were 133.1 million, average daily active users were 52.7 million, and DAU/MAU was 39.6%. DAUs grew 30% year over year, faster than MAUs at 14%, indicating increased usage frequency.
- Monetization: paid subscribers reached 12.2 million at year-end, up 28% from 9.5 million. Approximately 9% of MAUs were paid subscribers. Subscription bookings rose 36% to $996.3 million, and total bookings rose 33% to $1.158 billion.
- Financial growth: 2025 revenue was $1.038 billion, versus $748.0 million in 2024, an increase of approximately 39%. Operating income was $135.6 million, compared with $62.6 million.
- Cash generation: operating cash flow was $387.8 million and reported free cash flow was $360.4 million. Stock-based compensation was $137.4 million, so cash flow should not be treated as fully equivalent to owner earnings without considering dilution.
- Balance sheet: cash and cash equivalents were $1.036 billion, with another $239.2 million of short- and long-term investments. No borrowing debt was reported. Operating-lease liabilities totaled approximately $101.0 million and are analyzed separately from financial debt.
- Share evidence: the 2025 diluted weighted-average share count was 48.308 million, versus 45.773 million basic weighted-average shares. Year-end Class A and Class B shares outstanding totaled 46.628 million. The diluted count included founder awards, stock options, and restricted stock units.
- Earnings-quality caution: 2025 net income of $414.1 million included a one-time $256.7 million income-tax benefit from releasing a deferred-tax valuation allowance. Reported net income therefore overstates recurring operating profitability.
- Distribution concentration: 62% of revenue came through the Apple App Store and 20% through Google Play. Duolingo generally paid those platforms 15%-30% of payments processed through their in-app systems, creating platform-policy and take-rate risk.
- Management's metric definitions matter: MAUs and DAUs are period averages, while paid subscribers are measured at period end. Comparisons must preserve those definitions rather than treating all three as point-in-time counts.

## Consequential assumptions

- Product-led acquisition remains durable: user growth and engagement continue to arise primarily from product quality, retention, and word of mouth rather than increasingly expensive paid marketing.
- Engagement converts into economics: higher DAU/MAU ultimately supports paid conversion, retention, advertising inventory, or pricing power rather than engagement that is costly but weakly monetized.
- Subscription growth remains profitable after app-store fees, content and AI infrastructure costs, stock-based compensation, and dilution.
- Reported user and subscriber metrics are consistently measured and are sufficiently resistant to duplicate accounts, changing definitions, and product-mix effects.
- The cash balance is available to shareholders or productive reinvestment and is not consumed by acquisitions or spending that earns below the fund's required return.
- A suitable purchase price must provide an adequate return under a material deceleration scenario; business quality alone is not enough.

## Unknowns

- DUOL's share price, market capitalization, enterprise value, and valuation multiples on September 1, 2026.
- Results and guidance released after the 2025 year-end, including current user growth, subscriber growth, bookings, margins, cash flow, and dilution.
- Subscriber retention, churn, cohort behavior, family-plan economics, conversion by geography, and the contribution of price increases versus subscriber volume.
- Unit economics, including customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, incremental contribution margin, and the direct cost of serving AI-intensive features such as Duolingo Max.
- How much of recent engagement growth is durable versus driven by temporary product launches, marketing, notifications, or changes in metric measurement.
- Revenue and gross-profit concentration by product, geography, and platform, including the economics of Duolingo Max and the Duolingo English Test.
- Management's capital-allocation priorities for the cash and investment balance.
- The market's current growth and margin expectations and the downside if Duolingo merely performs well rather than exceptionally.

## Research plan

1. Establish the valuation-date capital structure using the September 1, 2026 closing price, current basic shares, in-the-money options and awards, cash, investments, debt, and lease liabilities. Reconcile weighted-average diluted shares to period-end fully diluted shares.
2. Read all 2026 10-Qs, earnings releases, call transcripts, and relevant 8-Ks available by the valuation date. Build a quarterly history of MAUs, DAUs, DAU/MAU, paid subscribers, bookings, revenue, gross margin, operating margin, stock-based compensation, and free cash flow using consistent definitions.
3. Decompose growth into user growth, paid conversion, pricing, retention, advertising, and other products. Identify where public disclosure cannot support the decomposition and avoid false precision.
4. Evaluate operating leverage by comparing revenue growth with cost of revenue, research and development, and sales and marketing. Adjust earnings and cash flow for the one-time tax benefit and explicitly account for stock-based compensation and dilution.
5. Assess competitive and platform risks: compare Duolingo's engagement, pricing, learning efficacy, AI features, and distribution economics with credible substitutes; test sensitivity to Apple and Google fees or policy changes.
6. Build base, upside, and downside five-year scenarios for subscribers, average revenue per subscriber, non-subscription revenue, margins, reinvestment, dilution, and terminal valuation. Use reverse valuation to identify what the current price already assumes.
7. Define decision thresholds before recommending initiation: required expected return, acceptable downside, evidence that growth remains product-led, and observable conditions that would invalidate the thesis.

## Partner-generated falsification question

What evidence would falsify the thesis if Duolingo's paid-subscriber growth fell below 20% while sales-and-marketing spending rose faster than revenue, indicating that growth increasingly depends on paid acquisition rather than durable product-led conversion?
